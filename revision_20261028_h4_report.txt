METHODS
Day-level correctness was analyzed using binomial generalized estimating equations clustered by participant. Prespecified sensitivity analyses crossed independence and exchangeable working correlations with unadjusted and scenario-, weekend-, and study-week-adjusted specifications. All six observed scenarios, including other, were retained; 18 days without a scenario label were excluded from scenario-adjusted analyses and retained in unadjusted summaries. A separate sensitivity model treated missing scenario labels as a category. Weekend status was verified against the calendar dates encoded in EMA prompt identifiers. Participant-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals used 2,000 resamples. Conditional logistic regression with participant fixed effects was used to assess within-participant associations among participants with variation in correctness.

RESULTS
Across 4,947 extension-sample person-days, unadjusted accuracy was 0.622 during examination weeks (1,287 days) and 0.619 otherwise (3,660 days); balanced accuracy was 0.634 and 0.621, respectively. The difference in balanced accuracy was 0.013 (participant-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [-0.042, 0.068]). The adjusted examination-week odds ratio for correctness was 0.805 (95% CI [0.666, 0.974], p=0.0254) with an exchangeable working correlation and 0.999 (95% CI [0.743, 1.344], p=0.9947) with independence. These population-averaged GEE coefficients are specification-dependent and do not establish a uniform decline in marginal performance during examinations. Participant fixed-effects analysis estimated a conditional odds ratio of 0.397 (95% CI [0.277, 0.568]) among 78 informative participants. Failure to reject a difference was not interpreted as evidence of equivalence.
